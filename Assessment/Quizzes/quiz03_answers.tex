\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=0.5em, label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
\key{B}{``Professional'' is compatible with a lawyer's register, a nurse's and a consultant's --- all
  different documents. The instruction does not narrow the distribution much, so the output is
  averaged and varies between runs.}
\key{B}{Field is subject vocabulary; tenor is the relationship and social distance; mode is the
  channel. ``You must submit'' and ``would it be possible'' differ in tenor.}
\key{C}{An adjective labels a feeling; a behaviour is something the text does, and can be checked
  against the output.}
\key{C}{Empty cells are unlikely continuations --- tables in training data are mostly full --- so
  something plausible fills the gap.}
\key{B}{The fallback principle: you are not appealing to honesty, you are making the honest answer
  the probable one by giving it a fixed, likely form.}
\key{C}{Negative constraints work when they are specific and countable. ``Do not be boring'' is a
  complaint, not a constraint.}
\key{B}{Next-token prediction where the tokens happen to be code. Everything from Week 3 applies,
  including that nothing checks correctness.}
\key{B}{Code is highly patterned, abundantly available, and common tasks recur almost identically.
  The corollary: unusual requirements sit on thin ground, delivered with identical confidence.}
\key{C}{Wrong logic with right appearance. Note that being wrong \emph{consistently} makes it look
  more reliable, not less --- a randomly failing tool would be caught.}
\key{B}{The verification gap. The surface is not evidence, and with generated code your usual
  defence --- reading the output --- is unavailable.}
\end{enumerate}
