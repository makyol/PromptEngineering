\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=0.5em, label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
\key{C}{Generative AI produces new content. Deciding whether a transaction is suspicious is
  classification; ordering scans is ranking; spam filtering is classification.}
\key{B}{A prompt is everything you supply, including attached files and images. This matters for
  Week 12: if you think the prompt is only what you typed, you will not anticipate indirect
  injection.}
\key{B}{Context is everything visible to the model at the moment of answering: your message, the
  conversation so far, attachments, and hidden system instructions. There is no memory outside it.}
\key{B}{Displacement --- correct for another country or context, with nothing fabricated and
  nothing applicable. This is the most common failure for work in Turkey.}
\key{A}{Fabrication --- a specific invented detail inside an otherwise correct answer. The format
  of a citation is highly patterned and easy to imitate; the content is not.}
\key{C}{Omission. There is no false statement to identify, so it can only be caught by someone who
  already knows what should have been said --- which is precisely what you lack outside your own
  expertise.}
\key{B}{ELIZA had no knowledge, no memory and no model of the conversation, yet people confided in
  it. Fluency alone produced the sense of understanding.}
\key{B}{The ELIZA effect, named in 1966 and still the most common error people make with these
  tools.}
\key{B}{The base rate for this field: reasonable direction, badly optimistic timing. Answer A
  overstates it --- the lesson is about calibration, not dismissal.}
\key{C}{Every major shift moved work away from human specification and towards learning from data.
  This is the most reliable trend line in the field's history.}
\end{enumerate}
