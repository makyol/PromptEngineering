**Course objective**

> To enable students from any discipline to work competently and critically with
> generative AI and large language models. The course develops the judgment required to
> assess whether an AI-generated answer is accurate, applicable and trustworthy —
> particularly outside the student's own area of expertise — and the practical ability to
> ground an AI system in selected sources, design multi-step workflows, and recognise and
> mitigate security and privacy risks. No programming background is required; all tools
> are browser-based and free.

**Learning outcomes**

> 1. Create clear, specific and context-aware prompts tailored to different domains and
>    tasks.
> 2. Apply structured prompting strategies, including role, format, constraint and
>    example-based techniques.
> 3. Assess AI responses for accuracy, bias, tone and relevance, and iteratively improve
>    results.
> 4. Apply prompting techniques across text, image, code and data contexts, with
>    discipline-specific adaptations.
> 5. Ground an AI system in selected source documents and verify that its answers are
>    genuinely supported by those sources rather than plausible-sounding.
> 6. Design and evaluate multi-step AI workflows and automations, and judge when a single
>    prompt is more appropriate than an automated chain.
> 7. Identify prompt injection, jailbreaking and data-leakage risks, and apply
>    user-level defences when handling institutional, personal or client data.

*(1–4 carried forward from the previous course information form; 5–7 are new.)*

**Course Content**

> This course covers the principles and practical application of prompt engineering for
> generative AI and large language models. Students first build a conceptual foundation:
> the history and development of AI, natural language processing and large language
> models; how these models process language; context windows, tokenization and why
> hallucination occurs; the principles of effective prompt design; and control of style,
> tone and format. The second half is applied: students build browser-based tools without
> writing code, ground AI systems in their own source documents, design verification
> procedures to detect incorrect output, construct multi-step automations, and adapt their
> work to the conventions of their own discipline. The course concludes with prompt
> injection and AI security, and with ethics, copyright, academic integrity and
> responsible use. Examples are drawn from healthcare, law, education, business and
> engineering throughout.
